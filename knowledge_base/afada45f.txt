The trends I’m describing don’t require a major breakthrough in AI. They’re starting to happen now, and they’ll happen more often as AIs get better at autonomous problem-solving. And it will involve far more than computer code. AI will hack all sorts of social, economic and political systems. It will find vulnerabilities and exploit them. This is a difference in kind, not just degree. We’re not ready for it, and we need to think about the implications now. First, I want to broaden the term “hacking.” The tax code isn’t computer code, but it is code. It’s a series of algorithms with inputs and outputs. It has vulnerabilities, which we call loopholes, and exploits, which we call tax avoidance strategies. Black hat hackers exploit them for profit. We call them accountants and attorneys.
