Keplr uses a company called Web3Auth to run that Google and Apple sign-in. The attacker used a security flaw at that company to reach the wallets, both companies said late last week. Web3Auth then used the attacker's own route to empty other at-risk wallets before the attacker got to them, it said. It moved that money, with help from the wallet maker, into recovery wallets for safekeeping. So an empty balance may mean the money was rescued, not stolen.
