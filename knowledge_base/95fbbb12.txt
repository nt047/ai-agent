2.4% of AFRINIC’s IPv4 addresses answer from outside Africa, and about 81% of those sit in one company’s four blocks
<!-- SC_OFF --><div class="md"><p>I work at IPinfo and this one started with a simple question: if a registry says an address is African, is it actually in Africa?</p> <p>This started as work for a paper by our research team (WHEREIS, IMC 2026). We tested it with ping times. Light in fiber only travels so far in a millisecond, so a fast reply puts a hard limit on how far away an address can be.</p> <p>Most registries pass, but AFRINIC is the exception: 2.4% of the addresses we could measure answer from outside Africa. And about 81% of those are in just four blocks, held by one company.</p> <p>What surprised me most while writing this is that the registries’ own files still list 98.6% of them as African. So anything that blocks or allows traffic by registry country gets these wrong.</p> <p>The numbers are a minimum, and anycast is left out. </p> </div><!-- SC_ON --> &#32;
