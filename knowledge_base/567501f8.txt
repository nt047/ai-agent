Last year, Character.AI asked a federal court in Florida to dismiss a wrongful-death suit, brought by the mother of a fourteen-year-old boy who had killed himself after months of conversation with its chatbot. The company made two arguments. One, that the chatbot’s words were speech that its users had a First Amendment right to receive. And two, that Character.AI was a service rather than a product—so product-liability law could not touch it. In May 2025, Judge Anne Conway accepted neither. Character.AI’s maneuver is part of a larger pattern. When the parents of a sixteen-year-old sued OpenAI over his suicide, the company’s answer also pleaded the First Amendment: the claims would restrain protected speech.
